## CWCStudy.jsx

1. **Subtitle: "— The Hague, 1993"** → CWC was opened for signature in **Paris**, 13 Jan 1993 (the OPCW is in The Hague). ZH subtitle "海牙" is wrong too. **H**. https://www.armscontrol.org/factsheets/cwcglance
2. **Stat "OPCW inspections in China: 500+"** and text "accepted over 500 routine and challenge inspections" → China's MFA says **750 on-site inspections as of June 2026** (it said 710 as of Mar 2025). This is a Chinese government figure; the OPCW publishes no per-country cumulative count. Use "~750 (per PRC MFA, June 2026)". **H** (that this is the figure China claims). https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/wjb/zzjg_663340/jks_665232/kjlc_665236/shwq_665244/202406/t20240606_11405161.html
3. **"routine and challenge inspections"**: no challenge inspection has **ever** been requested under the CWC, in China or anywhere. Delete "and challenge". The next sentence ("has not been subject to any formal challenge inspection") contradicts it. **H**. https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2003-04/news/six-year-old-cwc-passes-some-tests-fails-others (and later OPCW Review Conference statements)
4. **"the only case where an external body physically enters China to verify adherence"** (FrameworkBox; ZH same) → wrong. IAEA inspectors verify selected Chinese facilities under the 1988 Voluntary Offer Agreement and the Additional Protocol (see IAEA section). **H**.
5. **"allows independent inspectors to enter… without prior notice in some circumstances"** → the CWC always requires advance notice: at least 12 hours before arrival at the point of entry for challenge inspections, and longer for routine ones. Say "short-notice". **H**. CWC Verification Annex Part II(C), Part X: https://www.opcw.org/chemical-weapons-convention/annexes/verification-annex
6. **"State Department compliance reports (most recently 2021) … whether China may have retained a small offensive CW research programme"** → out of date and mischaracterised. The most recent report I could verify is the **April 2025 Condition (10)(C) report** (current to 31 Dec 2024). It says: "the United States cannot certify that China has met its obligations under the Convention due to concerns regarding China's research of pharmaceutical-based agents (PBAs) and toxins with potential dual-use applications." It does not allege a retained offensive programme. (Pre-2021 reports said China "may not have declared the full extent" of past CW activities.) I could not find a 2026 edition: UNVERIFIED. **H**. https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Condition-10-c-Report-Accessible-4.23.2025.pdf
7. **"Negotiated in the early 1990s"** → negotiated at the Conference on Disarmament from 1980 and concluded in 1992. Say "concluded in 1992 after more than a decade of negotiation". **M**.
8. **"an estimated 700,000 munitions"** → this is Japan's estimate. China has put the number at about 2 million, and ~330,000 at Haerbaling alone. Say "Japan estimates ~700,000; China's estimates are higher". **M**. https://wwwa.cao.go.jp/acw/en/keii/keii.html
9. **MoU date**: EN "1999" is correct (signed Beijing, 30 July 1999). **ZH says 2009: wrong.** A second MoU followed in 2012. **H**. https://nuke.fas.org/guide/japan/cw/730.htm
10. **"(Article 3 and the Annex on Implementation)"** → the ACW provisions are Art. I(3), Art. II(6), Art. III(1)(b)(iii) and Verification Annex Part IV(B). **M**.
11. **"Regulations on the Control of Export of Dual-Use Chemicals and Related Equipment and Technology (2002)"** → the 2002 instrument is the *Measures on Export Control of Certain Chemicals and Related Equipment and Technologies* (MOFTEC, Oct 2002). The CWC Schedules were brought into Chinese law by the *Regulations on Administration of Controlled Chemicals* (1995, rev. 2011). The current framework is the Export Control Law (2020) plus the Dual-Use Items Export Control Regulations (in force 1 Dec 2024). **M**. https://nti.org/?p=26584

## IAEAStudy.jsx

1. **"signed a comprehensive safeguards agreement in 1989"** → China, as an NPT nuclear-weapon state, has a **Voluntary Offer Agreement** (INFCIRC/369), **signed 20 Sept 1988**, in force 18 Sept 1989. Under it the IAEA applies safeguards only to facilities China offers and the IAEA selects. **H**. https://www.iaea.org/node/7352
2. **Subtitle "NPT Safeguards Agreement & Additional Protocol — Vienna, 1972 / 1998"** and **stat "IAEA Safeguards: 1972 (antecedent)"** → 1972 is the date of the generic INFCIRC/153 model, which does not apply to China. Use "Voluntary Offer Safeguards Agreement (1988) & Additional Protocol (signed Dec 1998, in force Mar 2002)". The AP stat "2002" is OK. **H**.
3. **FrameworkBox and text: US "conditioning access to civilian nuclear technology on IAEA certification" / "demand… IAEA certification" / "The treaty body (IAEA) provided the certification function"** → wrong. The 1985 agreement was held up by **presidential certifications to Congress** required by P.L. 99-183 (1985), plus the post-Tiananmen P.L. 101-246. **Clinton certified on 12 Jan 1998**, after China pledged to end nuclear cooperation with Iran. The agreement was implemented on 18 Mar 1998. The IAEA had no certification role. **H**. https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL33192.html
4. **"refused to activate… for nearly a decade"** → about 12.7 years (July 1985 – March 1998). **H**. Same CRS source.
5. **"Under US law, such agreements cannot be activated unless the partner accepts IAEA monitoring"** → AEA §123 full-scope-safeguards conditions do not apply to nuclear-weapon states in the same way. The block came from the joint resolution's certifications. **M**. CRS RL33192.
6. **"NPT accession followed in 1992 — relatively late for a nuclear weapon state (the US, USSR, UK had ratified in 1968–70)"** → this omits that **France also acceded in 1992** (Aug 1992), so China was not uniquely late. Also, China *acceded*; the stat label "NPT ratified" should read "acceded". **H**. https://treaties.unoda.org/t/npt
7. **"Civil Nuclear Facility Safety Supervision Regulations (1993)"** → promulgated by the State Council on **29 Oct 1986**. ZH says 1993 too. **H**. https://www.iaea.org/node/190611 (background), or the NNSA regulations list
8. **"Atomic Energy Law (still being drafted…)"**, **"process is still ongoing as of 2024"** → the Atomic Energy Law was **adopted by the NPCSC on 12 Sept 2025** (62 articles) and has been **in force since 15 Jan 2026**. **H**. https://npcobserver.com/legislation/atomic-energy-law/
9. **"Nuclear Safety Law (2017)"** → OK: adopted 1 Sept 2017, in force 1 Jan 2018.
10. **"China accepts routine inspections of its declared civil nuclear facilities"** → overstated. Under the VOA only facilities the IAEA selects from China's eligible list are inspected, which is a small number. **M**.
11. **"an estimated 500+ warheads"** (ZH 500枚以上) → SIPRI Yearbook 2026: **~620 warheads (Jan 2026)**. Pentagon CMPR (Dec 2025): "low 600s" operational, on track for >1,000 by 2030. **H**. https://fas.org/publication/the-pentagons-slimmed-down-2025-china-military-power-report/ ; SIPRI 2026 via https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3356269/china-adds-warheads-nuclear-powers-walk-away-disarmament-sipri
12. **"US intelligence assessments have flagged ongoing Chinese assistance to Pakistan's nuclear programme, though below the threshold that would trigger IAEA action"** → UNVERIFIED and no source given. Chinese reactor exports to Pakistan (Chashma, K-2/K-3) are under IAEA safeguards. The real dispute is over NSG guidelines, and the IAEA has no mandate over export policy. Remove, or source it to a specific report. **M**.
13. **"development of tactical nuclear weapons"** → speculative. The DoD mentions possible low-yield systems. Hedge it. **L**.
14. **"China joined the IAEA in 1984"** → OK (Jan 1984). **"NNSA… 1984"** → OK.

## MontrealStudy.jsx

1. **Rigby et al. figures** (≈7,000 t/yr more in 2014–17 than in 2008–12; 40–60% of the global increase; Shandong and Hebei) → **correct**. Rigby et al. 2019, *Nature* 569:546–550. **H**. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1193-4
2. **"By 2019 Chinese CFC-11 emissions had returned to expected levels"** → consistent with **Park et al. 2021**, *Nature* 590:433–437 (eastern-China emissions in 2019 were about 10 Gg/yr lower than in 2014–17, back to pre-2013 levels). Cite Park. **H**. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03277-w
3. **"China received over $440 million for its CFC phase-out programme"** → $440m is the World Bank's figure for MLF grants to its **Fourth ODS Phase-out Project (1997–2013)**, which was multi-sector (CFC, halon and others). The World Bank project database lists the grant as $350m. It is not "the CFC programme" total. **M**. https://projects.worldbank.org/en/projects-operations/project-detail/P039838
4. **"a further $385 million for the subsequent HCFC phase"** → $385m (approved Apr 2013) is the ceiling for **HCFC production-sector** phase-out to 2030 only. HCFC consumption plans were funded separately (Stage I alone was $265m, July 2011). **H**. https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/un-support-china-phase-out-ozone-depleting-substances-major-climate
5. **Stat "Multilateral Fund received: $800m+"** → this is just $440m + $385m. China's cumulative MLF approvals are larger. Exact total UNVERIFIED (check MLF Secretariat country data). **M**.
6. **"last six CFC plants were closed on 1 July 2007 — two and a half years ahead of the 2010 deadline"** → correct (World Bank). **H**.
7. **"the Protocol's trade provisions meant non-compliance would block Chinese manufacturers…"** → wrong. Art. 4 trade measures target **non-Parties**; non-compliant Parties face the non-compliance procedure, not trade bans. **H**. https://ozone.unep.org/treaties/montreal-protocol/articles/article-4-control-trade-non-parties
8. **"China acceded in 1991… partly because as a developing country it was initially outside the core negotiating group"** → the documented reason is that China waited for the 1990 London Amendment, which created the Multilateral Fund. Accession was June 1991. **M**. Zhao 2002 (ref below).
9. **"By the early 1990s China had become the world's largest producer and consumer of CFCs"** → China's production peaked in 1998 (55,000 t). "Largest producer" fits the mid/late 1990s. **M**. https://news.un.org/en/node/224422
10. **"atmospheric scientists detected [in 2018]"** → OK: Montzka et al. 2018, *Nature* 557:413.

## WTOStudy.jsx

1. **"has since blocked WTO reform proposals — most notably on dispute settlement"** → wrong actor. The **US** has blocked Appellate Body appointments since 2017–19. China co-sponsors the AB-appointment proposal and joined the MPIA (2020). China's resistance has been on SOE/subsidy disciplines and developing-country status. **H**.
2. **"The USTR's first two-year WTO compliance review (2004)"** → the 2004 report is by **Terence P. Stewart (Stewart & Stewart) for the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission**, 19 Mar 2004. USTR's own reports have been annual since Dec 2002. The 2004 report also graded China "no better than C" overall, while confirming timely tariff cuts. **H**. https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/Research/china%20compliance%20with%20wto%20obligations%20first%20two%20years.pdf
3. **"China revised or repealed over 3,000 laws…" / stat "3,000+"** → Xue & Jin (2009) do say ">3000… repealed, abrogated, revised, enacted". The official 2018 State Council white paper says **~2,300 laws, regulations and departmental rules at central level** and **~190,000 sub-central measures**. Use the white paper. **M**. https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/201806/28/content_WS5d16bf89c6d03ce678793b0c.html
4. **"Uniquely… WTO agreements were not published in the NPC Standing Committee Gazette"** → UNVERIFIED, and the cited Xue & Jin article does not say it. "Uniquely" is almost certainly wrong, since many treaties are not gazetted. The narrower point, that courts apply transforming domestic law and not WTO texts, is supported by the SPC's 2002 Provisions on international trade administrative cases (Art. 9). **L/M**.
5. **"respondent in approximately 50 WTO cases — the most of any country alongside the US and EU"** → about 49–50 is right (Gao & Ji: 49 as respondent, 24 as complainant, c. Apr 2025; the EU filed further cases in 2025–26). It is **third** after the US and EU, not tied with them. **M**. https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/countries_e/china_e.htm
6. **"approximately 25 cases… as complainant, mostly the US"** → OK (24–26). **M**.
7. **"has appealed into the WTO's now-dysfunctional Appellate Body in several cases"** → OK if it means appealing "into the void": China did so in the solar safeguards case (2021) and the Section 232 retaliation case (2023). Note that it also uses the MPIA with other MPIA members. **M**. https://www.caixinglobal.com/2021-09-22/china-appeals-wto-decision-upholding-trump-solar-panel-tariffs-101775928.html
8. **"China took 15 years to report its first subsidy through a local government"** → matches USTR (no sub-central subsidy notification until July 2016). The wording should say "notify sub-central subsidies". **M**.
9. **USTR "state-directed, non-market" model** → USTR's wording is "state-led, non-market" policies and practices. Fix the quote. **M**.

## NotableAbsencesStudy.jsx (+ NOTABLE_ABSENCES in src/data/constants.js)

1. **ICCPR "never ratified in the 27 years since"** → signed 5 Oct 1998. As of Oct 2026 that is **28 years**, still unratified. Use "nearly three decades" so it doesn't go stale. The constants card says "27+", which is OK. **H**. https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-4&chapter=4
2. **constants.js: Hague Apostille Convention listed as an absence with a "Not party" badge** → China acceded on 8 Mar 2023 and it has been in force since 7 Nov 2023. The card's own note says so, but it should be removed from "absences" or relabelled. **H**. https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41
3. **constants.js: OECD Anti-Bribery: "Not a party (non-OECD member)"** → non-OECD states can and do join (e.g. Argentina, Brazil, Bulgaria, South Africa), so non-membership is not the reason. **M**.
4. Context worth adding: China **ratified ILO C29 and C105 (forced labour) in April 2022**, while C87/C98 remain unratified. This sharpens the "independent unions" point. **H**.

## Developments since mid-2025 worth adding

- **Atomic Energy Law** adopted 12 Sept 2025, in force 15 Jan 2026 (IAEA). https://npcobserver.com/legislation/atomic-energy-law/
- **Warheads**: Pentagon CMPR (23 Dec 2025) gives "low 600s" and >1,000 by 2030, and dropped the 1,500-by-2035 projection. SIPRI Yearbook 2026 gives ~620 (Jan 2026).
- **CWC**: April 2025 Condition (10)(C) report still "cannot certify" (PBAs/toxins). PRC reports 750 OPCW inspections (June 2026). Japan's destruction plan targets end-2027 for stored ACWs (excluding Haerbaling). https://www.mofa.go.jp/dns/b_cwc/pageite_000001_00001.html
- **WTO**: China pledged (Li Qiang, 23 Sept 2025) **not to seek new special and differential treatment** in current or future WTO negotiations. https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1824406-20250924.htm . The USTR 2025 China WTO compliance report came out in March 2026. The EU brought new WTO cases against China in 2025–26 (patent licensing / anti-suit injunctions).
- **Absences**: ICCPR is still unratified (28 years). The Apostille Convention should be dropped from the list.
- **Montreal** (pre-2025 but missing): China accepted the **Kigali Amendment** (in force for China 15 Sept 2021), which is relevant to the "ongoing distributed obligations" argument.
