## 1. blog-text.md

### Section 1: How treaties work

- **"around 560 major multilateral treaties and hundreds of thousands of bilateral ones"**
  - The 560 figure is fine. The UN says "over 560" multilateral treaties are deposited with the Secretary-General. Note that the About page says "~570"; pick one figure.
  - "Hundreds of thousands of bilateral" is overstated. Hoffman et al. say "at least 250,000 treaties exist". The UNTS figure (~250k) counts treaties *and subsequent actions*, mostly bilateral.
  - Suggested wording: "...and roughly 250,000 treaties and related actions registered with the UN, most of them bilateral."
  - Confidence: medium.
  - Sources: https://www.un.org/ruleoflaw/?p=10403 ; https://www.yorku.ca/news/2022/08/02/do-international-treaties-actually-work-york-led-study-says-they-mostly-dont/
- **"[Add data on treaty types]"** — suggested fill:
  - The MTDSG groups the ~560 SG-deposited treaties into 29 subject chapters (human rights, refugees, narcotics, trade, transport, environment, disarmament, law of the sea, etc.).
  - Hoffman et al. 2022 classify treaties into 6 domains: environment, human rights, humanitarian, maritime, security, trade/finance.
  - Xue & Jin (2009): China had "ratified or acceded to more than 200 multilateral treaties" since 1978.
  - MFA annual "条约情况" reports say China concludes roughly 100–300 bilateral treaty-type documents a year (e.g. "300余项" in 2023, "近200项" in 2025).
  - Sources: https://treaties.un.org/pages/ParticipationStatus.aspx ; PNAS PMC9372541 ; https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjb_673085/zfxxgk_674865/gknrlb/tywj/tyqk/202605/t20260514_11910416.shtml
- **Stratechery quote** — OK. "Anthropic and Alignment", Ben Thompson, 2 Mar 2026; the quote matches. https://stratechery.com/2026/anthropic-and-alignment/
- **"France signing & ratifying a human rights treaty immediately results in French courts being able to enforce it"**
  - Partly true. Art. 55 of the French Constitution gives ratified treaties authority above statute, subject to reciprocity. However, French courts apply only provisions with direct effect.
  - Suggest adding "most provisions".
  - Confidence: medium.
- **"(In fact, bilateral treaties are significantly more likely to be effective)"** — NOT supported by the meta-analysis.
  - Hoffman et al. *excluded* bilateral treaties ("treaties involving more than two countries").
  - What they found instead is a "suggestive dose–response relationship between increasing forum size and reduced impacts": Z = 4.36 for forums with ≤5 parties vs 1.12 for ≥100. They add that this is hard to disentangle from policy domain.
  - Suggested wording: "treaties negotiated among small groups of countries appear more effective."
  - Confidence: high.
  - Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9372541/
- **"there is good evidence (find) that whichever country or secretariat gets the first text written down has a disproportionate influence"**
  - Some evidence exists, but it is not a single definitive study. Suggested citations:
    - Allee & Lugg 2016, "Who wrote the rules for the Trans-Pacific Partnership?", *Research & Politics* 3(3), doi:10.1177/2053168016658919. A text-reuse study: TPP text was drawn disproportionately from prior US agreements, especially in the investment chapters. https://www.designoftradeagreements.org/media/filer_public/96/d4/96d4b0fd-bb1d-4b86-a074-9a88e71d3d9b/allee_and_lugg_rp_july_2016.pdf
    - Galinsky & Mussweiler 2001, "First offers as anchors", *JPSP* 81(4). Experimental evidence on first-mover anchoring in negotiation.
    - Depledge 2005, *The Organization of Global Negotiations* (UNFCCC chair/secretariat texts).
    - Biermann & Siebenhüner 2009, *Managers of Global Change* (secretariat influence).
  - I did not open the Galinsky, Depledge or Biermann works in this check; the citations are from my knowledge.
  - Suggest softening "good evidence" to "evidence from trade-agreement text analysis and negotiation experiments".
  - Confidence: medium.
- **Putnam "two-level game"**
  - The attribution is OK (Putnam 1988, *International Organization* 42(3)).
  - The paraphrase is loose. Putnam's point is that Level-I deals must fit within the domestic Level-II "win-set", and a constrained win-set can strengthen a negotiator's hand. Acceptable as written.
  - Confidence: high.
- **"(Add veto points here)" (twice)** — suggested fills:
  - Tsebelis's term is **veto players** (*Veto Players*, 2002). **Veto points** is Immergut's term (1990/1992). Fix the attribution in the next paragraph ("called a Veto point by George Tsebelis" → "a veto player in Tsebelis's terms", or "a veto point (Immergut)").
  - For China's implementation stage, the relevant veto players are:
    - drafting ministries
    - the State Council's legislative office (now under the Ministry of Justice)
    - the NPC Legislative Affairs Commission
    - provincial and local governments
  - This is the literature on "fragmented authoritarianism": Lieberthal & Oksenberg 1988; Mertha 2009, *China Quarterly* 200.
  - Confidence: high on the attribution; medium on the fill.
- **"In dualist systems like the UK or US, the treaty must be separately written into domestic law"**
  - Wrong for the US. Art. VI (the Supremacy Clause) makes treaties "the supreme Law of the Land", and self-executing treaties are enforceable without legislation (see *Medellín v. Texas*, 2008). The US is usually described as mixed or monist-leaning.
  - Keep the UK as the dualist example.
  - Confidence: high.
- **"The US requires a two-thirds Senate majority for ratification"**
  - Minor: the Senate gives advice and consent by two-thirds of senators present, and the President ratifies.
  - Confidence: high.
- **"the US has ratified roughly half the environmental treaties its European neighbours have"**
  - The source (Mazzega 2024, Frontiers in Political Science, "Environmental ratification moves") says "The USA has ratified half as many treaties as the European G7 states". It also says DE/FR/GB have ratified "almost three-quarters" while the US and Japan have ratified "less than half".
  - Change "European neighbours" to "the European G7 states (Germany, France, UK)".
  - Confidence: high.
  - Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2024.1388191/full
- **"typically completed within a single session of the [NPC Standing Committee](https://english.court.gov.cn/), around two to four months"**
  - The link is wrong: english.court.gov.cn is the **Supreme People's Court** site. Use http://www.npc.gov.cn/englishnpc/ or the Wikipedia NPCSC page.
  - "Two to four months" is UNVERIFIED and misleading. The NPCSC usually approves a treaty bill in one bimonthly session, but the signing-to-ratification lag ranges from months to years, or never. Examples:
    - Paris Agreement: signed 22 Apr 2016, approved 3 Sep 2016 (~4.5 months).
    - CWC: signed Jan 1993, ratified Dec 1996.
    - ICESCR: signed 1997, ratified 2001.
    - ICCPR (signed 1998) and CTBT (signed 1996): never ratified.
  - Confidence: high on the link; high that the timing claim is too general.

    - **Art. 7** says courts apply PRC laws, administrative regulations and local regulations.
    - **Art. 9** says that where a law or regulation admits two or more reasonable interpretations, courts shall choose the one consistent with China's treaties (except reserved provisions). This is a treaty-consistent interpretation rule, which cuts against the "it didn't last" framing.

  - Confidence: high.
  - Sources: https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/最高人民法院关于审理国际贸易行政案件若干问题的规定 ; Xue & Jin fn. 34.

The SPC issued 法释〔2023〕15号 (Interpretation on the Application of International Treaties and International Practice in Foreign-Related Civil and Commercial Cases, effective 1 Jan 2024). Its Art. 1 restores the rule that treaties prevail over conflicting PRC law in foreign-related civil/commercial cases, except reserved provisions. It was issued expressly to fill the gap left by the GPCL's repeal.
  - Confidence: high.

### Section 5: Patterns (meta-analysis)

- The meta-analysis is **Hoffman, Baral, ... Poirier (2022)**, "International treaties have mostly failed to produce their intended effects", *PNAS* 119(32): e2122854119. It covers 224 studies, with a meta-analysis of 82.
- Name and cite it: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9372541/
- Bullet-by-bullet:
  - **"Including enforcement mechanisms within treaties led to increased effectiveness"** — OK as the paper's own phrasing (subgroup Z = 3.13). However, in meta-regression enforcement was "not [an] independent driver" (β = 0.04, CI −2.19 to 2.28). Add the caveat. Confidence: high.
  - **"Transparency, complaint, and oversight mechanisms were very marginally effective"** — WRONG. The paper says they "did not" show significant effects / "were not associated with greater treaty effectiveness". Say "showed no significant effect". Confidence: high.
